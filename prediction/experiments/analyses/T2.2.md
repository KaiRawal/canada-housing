# T2.2 — Target formulation study: level vs Δlog vs YoY%

**Date:** 2026-08-26 · **Commit stage:** `exp(T2.2)` · **Runs logged:** `T2.2-level`, `T2.2-dlog`, `T2.2-yoy` in runs.jsonl

## Decision rule (stated up front, before results were seen)

- **Primary criterion:** a target form is adopted only if it improves MDA over
  persistence (paired same-mask folds) **without sacrificing point-error
  class** (NRMSE/NMAE within ~persistence's scale, i.e. not the 6–18× regime
  of drift/ARIMA).
- **Secondary criterion:** stability of the improvement across the 5 folds
  (no single-fold heroics).
- If no form satisfies the primary criterion → keep **level** (simplest,
  inversion-free) and record that directional skill must come from elsewhere.

## What was done

`prediction/experiments/target_formulation.py` runs the T2.1 pooled
Ridge(α=1)-on-lags test vehicle (all 176 lag-1 features + CMA dummies,
StandardScaler inside a Pipeline fitted on the TRAIN WINDOW ONLY — reused
verbatim from `baselines_suite.ridge_lags_model_fn`) under three target forms:

| Form | Training target | Inversion to level space |
|---|---|---|
| level | y_t | identity |
| dlog | log(y_t) − log(y_{t−1}) | ŷ_t = exp(log(y_{t−1}) + pred), anchored on **actual** y_{t−1} |
| yoy | 100·(y_t/y_{t−12} − 1) | ŷ_t = y_{t−12}·(1 + pred/100), anchored on **actual** y_{t−12} |

Transforms and anchors are computed by within-CMA backward `shift(1)` /
`shift(12)` over the train split only — purely past-looking by construction.
The Δlog/YoY inversions use one-step-refreshed ACTUAL anchors: exactly the
information persistence conditions on (and the information already present in
ridge's lag-1 features), so all competitors share one information set. No
future info beyond what persistence itself uses. Rows whose anchor is missing
(a late starter's first in-window month(s)) yield NaN predictions and drop out
via the harness common mask — identically for model and paired baseline.

Scoring goes through the SAME harness path as every other run
(`_score_fold`: common-mask rows, boundary-anchored MDA via `_anchored_mda`,
`evaluation.calculate_all_metrics` for NMSE/NRMSE/NMAE), so model vs
persistence numbers are paired within-fold.

**Sanity checks (passed):**
1. `T2.2-level` reproduces `T2.1-ridge-lags` EXACTLY (MDA 0.370997… identical
   to full float precision) — confirms the study machinery is the T2.1 setup.
2. Persistence-in-level-space IS the zero-Δlog / zero-YoY forecast, so a
   separate "persistence-on-transformed-target" run would be tautological;
   Δlog direction ≡ level direction by definition (sign of Δy). Not re-run.

## Verdict: KEEP THE LEVEL TARGET

> **Correction (2026-08-26, folded into T2.3):** the "REFUTED" wording below was
> too strong on one narrow point. A direct-sign diagnostic (T2.3 critic fix #1;
> runs.jsonl rows `T2.2-directsign-dlog` / `T2.2-directsign-yoy`,
> `artifacts/T2.2/direct_sign_diagnostic.json`) shows the raw Δlog / YoY heads
> DO carry genuine above-chance directional signal — sign(Δlog pred) matches
> sign(Δy_true) on 0.6076±0.0848 of directionally-active months (YoY 0.5808) —
> but that signal is WEAKER than the momentum reference
> (sign(y_{t−1}−y_{t−2}): 0.678) and than the same ridge predicting the level
> directly, so no decision changes: Δlog/YoY produce NO gain under the
> canonical level-space MDA protocol and LEVEL stays the target. Also note all
> MDA numbers in this file are legacy-protocol values; corrected zero-drop-MDA
> rows were appended to runs.jsonl by T2.3 (`*-mdazero`) — see
> analyses/T2.3.md Part A.

No target form passes the primary criterion — none improves MDA over
persistence at all, let alone without sacrificing point errors:

- **Δlog does NOT fix the direction problem**: MDA moves from 0.3710 to
  0.3715 (+0.0005, deep inside fold noise; std ≈0.04). The T2.1 hypothesis
  that predicting monthly changes instead of levels would rescue directional
  accuracy is REJECTED under the canonical level-space MDA protocol — the raw
  Δlog head has real but inferior directional signal (correction note above),
  and it never translates into an MDA gain over persistence. Point errors
  also worsen slightly (NRMSE 0.00169 → 0.00197) because inversion compounds
  the small Δlog errors exponentially.
- **YoY% is strictly worse everywhere**: MDA −0.039 vs level, NRMSE 13×
  worse (0.0202). Regressing a 12-month cumulative change onto lag-1
  features both amplifies noise and mismatches the feature horizon.
- Level remains the best challenger formulation on every metric and needs no
  inversion machinery.

Interpretation tied to T2.1: the direction deficit (MDA ≈0.37 vs 0.46) is a
property of what pooled lag-feature regression DOES (smoothing across CMAs
and quarters), not of the units it predicts in — reformulating the target
algebra leaves the deficit intact. Directional skill must come from different
signal (spatial/temporal engineered features, T3), different conditioning
(recursive forecasting, T4.3), or accepting persistence-grade direction and
winning on point errors only.

## Pitfalls encountered

- First run crashed: the helper transform columns (`_lag1/_lag12/_dlog/_yoy`)
  leaked into the ridge feature matrix (NaN check fired). Fixed by stripping
  underscore-prefixed helper columns from features; they are anchors/labels
  only. The crash occurred BEFORE any `log_run`, so runs.jsonl stayed clean.
- YoY fold-5 mask shrinkage (251 vs 317 rows) is protocol-dependent; flagged
  explicitly rather than silently mixed into the aggregate comparison.
- statsmodels `.append(refit=False)` (used by the T2.1 recursive ARIMA fix
  folded into this commit) requires contiguous months per CMA; asserted.

## Sample sizes / fold dates

Train split: 7792 rows × 176 features, 24 CMAs, monthly 1981-01 → 2022-03.
Folds (expanding, anchor 2017): val 2008–09, 2010–11, 2012–13, 2014–15,
2016–17; folds 1–4 = 18 CMAs / 432 val rows; fold 5 = 24 CMAs / 323 val rows
(eval rows: 317 for level/dlog, 251 for yoy — see above). Training rows lost
to NaN transforms: dlog loses 24 rows (first month per CMA) per fold; yoy
loses 12 months × CMA count per fold. No test-split file was read anywhere in
this sub-stage.
