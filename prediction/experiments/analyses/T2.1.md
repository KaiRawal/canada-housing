## What was done

Three baselines beyond persistence were implemented in
`prediction/experiments/baselines_suite.py` and run through
`harness.evaluate()` on the identical 5 expanding folds (val windows
2008–09 … 2016–17, anchor 2017). Persistence is computed by the harness itself
on the same folds and same row masks as each model (`_score_fold` common-mask),
so every model-vs-persistence number below is paired within-fold.

All baselines forecast the **`total` index level** directly — that is what the
harness scores; the Δlog-vs-level question is studied separately in T2.2.

1. **Drift (random walk with drift):** per CMA, drift = mean first difference
   of the level over the fold's train window; `y(t) = y_last + h·drift`,
   h = months since the CMA's last train observation.
2. **Per-CMA ARIMA:** statsmodels `ARIMA(order=(1,1,0))`, fit per CMA on the
   train window only (~90 fits total, seconds of compute). Fixed small order
   chosen a priori to bound compute (no pmdarima installed; no search).
3. **Ridge-on-lags:** ONE pooled `Ridge(alpha=1.0)` across all CMAs on all 176
   existing `*_lag_1` features from `X_train_full_total.csv` plus CMA dummy
   columns, standardized by a `StandardScaler` inside an sklearn Pipeline fit
   on the train window only. Pooled-with-dummies chosen over per-CMA because
   early folds give each CMA only ~270 monthly rows against 176 features;
   whether spatial pooling truly helps is probed properly in T2.3.
   Late-starter CMAs unseen at train time are scored via dummy reindex(fill=0)
   using numeric lags alone (fold-5 eval rows = 317 for ridge vs 245 for
   drift/ARIMA, which cannot produce any prediction for those CMAs).

## Recursive-update variants (critic fix #2)

The original drift/ARIMA extrapolated from a FROZEN last-train anchor over up
to 24 months, while persistence/ridge refresh from actual y_{t−1} every month —
an information-set mismatch. Two truth-refreshed one-step-ahead variants were
added to `baselines_suite.py` and run on identical folds (registry rows
`T2.1-drift-recursive`, `T2.1-arima-recursive`; both use ONLY actuals through
t−1, exactly like persistence):

| Predictor (same-mask paired) | MDA | NMSE | NRMSE | NMAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| persistence [paired mask] | 0.4587 ± 0.0342 | 1.64e-06 ± 1.31e-06 | 0.00119 ± 0.00051 | 0.00066 ± 0.00027 |
| drift-recursive | **0.4624 ± 0.0404** | 1.60e-06 ± 1.29e-06 | **0.00116 ± 0.00051** | 0.00074 ± 0.00027 |
| arima(1,1,0)-recursive | 0.3843 ± 0.0394 | 3.81e-06 ± 3.08e-06 | 0.00178 ± 0.00080 | 0.00111 ± 0.00043 |

Read: recursive drift is statistically INDISTINGUISHABLE from persistence
(MDA delta +0.0037, wins folds 1/2/3, loses 4/5 at n=5; NRMSE marginally
better, NMAE marginally worse) — the estimated monthly drift adds nothing
once the anchor is refreshed. Recursive ARIMA is WORSE than its frozen
multi-horizon self (0.4343 → 0.3843): its one-step forecast
ŷ_t = y_{t−1} + φ·Δy_{t−1} systematically mis-signs small momentum terms.
Both variants cannot predict CMAs with no train history (fold-5 masks = 245
rows; their paired persistence blocks above reflect that same mask). The bar
remains persistence; recursive drift merely matches it.

> **Near-tautology caveat (T2.3 critic fix #6):** the recursive-drift tie with
> persistence is close to tautological, and its "wins" are weak corroboration
> only: the per-CMA estimated drift mu is TINY (monthly NHPI moves are ~0.0x
> index points), so ŷ_t = y_{t−1} + mu_c ≡ persistence + ε. Whenever
> |Δy_{t−1}| exceeds |mu| (most directionally-active months), recursive drift
> predicts EXACTLY the persistence direction by construction — agreement is
> baked in, not evidence of skill. Any drift-style challenger must be judged
> on whether it ever flips a direction, which here it essentially never does.

## Verdict: the bar

**Persistence wins every aggregate metric against every frozen-anchor
challenger.** Same-mask PAIRED deltas (model minus its own paired persistence,
identical eval rows per fold) and per-fold MDA sign pattern:

| Challenger | paired ΔMDA (agg) | per-fold MDA sign vs persistence |
|---|---|---|
| drift | −0.042 | loses 5/5 folds |
| arima(1,1,0) | −0.028 | wins fold 2 only (0.4838 > 0.4514); ties fold 3 (0.4120 = 0.4120); loses folds 1, 4, 5 → **persistence beats ARIMA on only 3/5 scored folds** |
| ridge-on-lags | −0.092 | loses 5/5 folds |
| drift-recursive | +0.004 | wins folds 1–3, loses 4–5 → tie at n=5 |

With n=5 folds these sign patterns support NO clean-win narration: ARIMA's
fold-2 edge (the only fold it wins) does not survive aggregation, and
recursive-drift's 3/5 record is a coin-flip-sized margin. The robust reading
is at the AGGREGATE level, where persistence's margins are:

- vs drift: MDA +0.042 absolute (+10% relative); point errors ≈6× smaller
  (NRMSE 0.00113 vs 0.00699).
- vs ARIMA(1,1,0): MDA +0.028 (+6.5% relative); point errors ≈7× smaller.
- vs ridge-on-lags: MDA +0.091 (+25% relative); point errors ≈1.5× smaller
  (NRMSE 0.00113 vs 0.00169, NMAE 0.00061 vs 0.00122).

Interpretation:

1. NHPI `total` is close to a martingale at monthly frequency: estimated drift
   and AR dynamics extrapolated over up-to-24-month horizons actively hurt.
   Refreshing the anchor monthly does not rescue them — recursive ARIMA is
   worse than frozen ARIMA, and recursive drift only reaches a tie with
   persistence. ARIMA's slight edge is confined to fold-2 MDA (0.4838 >
   0.4514); fold 5 shows none (0.4449 < 0.5020 same-mask).
2. Ridge-on-lags is the only challenger in persistence's error class on point
   metrics — pooled lag features carry real level information — but its
   directions are WORSE-than-chance-flavored (MDA 0.371 < 0.4625): smoothing
   across CMAs/quarters mispredicts month-to-month sign more often than
   copying yesterday does. Directional skill must come from somewhere else
   (target reformulation or genuinely different signal), which motivated T2.2.
3. Downstream definition: "beating the baseline" = beating **persistence**
   (MDA 0.4625±0.0394, NMSE 1.52e-06±1.27e-06, NRMSE 0.00113±0.00049,
   NMAE 0.00061±0.00024) on the same folds/masks. No simple time-series
   baseline comes close on direction.

## Pitfalls encountered

- First suite attempt crashed in the ARIMA predictor (positional-vs-label
  indexing of months); fixed with an index-aligned month Series. The crash
  left one duplicate `T2.1-drift` row in runs.jsonl; the claim that the
  duplicate was byte-equivalent to its re-run is **attestation-only** (the
  artifact had been overwritten before a byte comparison could be recorded).
  Going forward runs.jsonl is STRICTLY APPEND-ONLY: duplicates are kept in
  place and marked superseded in prose — never deleted.
- Drift/ARIMA cannot predict CMAs with no train history, so their fold-5
  evaluation set shrinks to 245 rows while ridge/persistence score 317; the
  leaderboard reports `fold5_eval_rows` explicitly and each run carries its
  own same-mask persistence block for fair paired comparison.
- statsmodels convergence warnings on short series suppressed inside the
  fitting context only; fits verified finite via downstream assertions.
